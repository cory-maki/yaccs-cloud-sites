What Is Generative Engine Optimization (GEO)?

📅 April 3, 2026
✎ Cory Maki
⌚ 9 min read

Generative Engine Optimization is the practice of earning citations in AI-generated search answers — the responses produced by ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, Gemini, and Microsoft Copilot. It is not a replacement for SEO. It is a layer that now sits on top of it.

Most working definitions of GEO start with what it is and ignore why it has to exist. The why is the harder half. AI Overviews now appear in over half of U.S. searches; for B2B technology queries, that figure rises to roughly 70%. When the AI answer appears, only about 8% of users click an organic result. And the AI answer itself names just three or four brands. Out of 18 million indexed domains, only around 274,000 have ever appeared in an AI Overview. The funnel narrowed before most teams noticed it was changing shape.

What GEO actually optimizes for

Classical SEO optimizes for ranked links: a query goes in, ten blue links come out, and the ranking algorithm decides the order. GEO optimizes for inclusion in a synthesized answer: a query goes in, a passage of generated text comes out, and somewhere inside that passage your brand is either named or it isn’t. The unit of selection is no longer the page. It’s the passage.

That distinction matters more than it sounds. Search engines used to evaluate documents holistically and present them whole. Generative engines extract — they pull short, factual passages from sources they trust and stitch them together. A page that ranks well but reads like an essay can be invisible to a system that wants a self-contained sentence it can quote. Citability is the new ranking layer, and it lives at the sentence level.

The unit of selection is no longer the page. It’s the passage.

The platforms behave differently

One reason a unified “AI SEO” approach falls apart in practice is that each platform has its own citation pattern. Google AI Overviews lean heavily on Wikipedia (around 47.9% of citations, per DemandSage’s 2026 dataset), Reddit (~21%), and YouTube (~18.8%). Perplexity is even more tilted toward Reddit — about 46.5% of its citations come from Reddit threads. ChatGPT Search uses Bing’s index, which means brands missing from Bing are effectively missing from ChatGPT regardless of how well they rank in Google. Gemini behaves differently again, splitting its source mix depending on whether the query is informational or recommendation-style.

A real GEO program treats these as separate problems. Bing indexation isn’t a footnote when ChatGPT is involved; it’s a precondition. Reddit isn’t a content channel; it’s the highest-leverage citation source on two of the largest engines. The strategy that earns you a citation in Perplexity will underperform in Gemini, and the inverse is also true. Pretending otherwise is how you spend a quarter moving numbers that don’t matter.

How a GEO engagement actually runs

The framework I use with clients is the ARC Method I developed for SaaS founders — Audit, Reddit & Reputation, Citability — across a 90-day window. The audit phase establishes baselines on each platform and identifies the specific queries where competitors appear and you don’t. The Reddit and reputation phase builds the off-site authority AI models actually weight, on the platforms they actually weight it on. Citability restructures the on-site content so the passages AI extracts are accurate, self-contained, and consistent across sources.

Cross-source consistency is the part teams underestimate most. AI systems synthesize across Wikipedia, LinkedIn, Crunchbase, your own site, and any number of third-party profiles. When those sources disagree about your founding date, your category, or your headcount, the model has to pick one — and it doesn’t always pick the one you’d want. The cleanup work is unglamorous and disproportionately powerful.

Conclusion

GEO is best understood as an evolution of SEO, not a revolution against it. The fundamentals — technical health, useful content, durable authority — still apply. What’s new is the answer-layer above them, and the small, specific set of techniques that decide whether your brand gets named in the answer or sits invisibly under it. If you’re a SaaS founder who has noticed competitors getting recommended by ChatGPT while your own brand isn’t, that’s the work to do next.
