The “GEO Is Just SEO” Debate — Where I Stand

📅 April 4, 2026
✎ Cory Maki
⌚ 8 min read

There are two loud camps in the GEO conversation right now. One says GEO is a revolution that obsoletes SEO. The other says GEO is just SEO with a marketing rebrand. Both are wrong, and the evidence makes the middle position obvious once you look at it for ten minutes.

Disclosure on framing: I have a book called Reddit, AI Overviews & GEO, so I’m obviously not neutral on whether GEO is a real discipline worth writing about. But I’m also openly skeptical of GEO tool vendors selling dashboards before the discipline is mature, and that skepticism is the actual reason this debate matters. The hype side and the dismissive side are both selling something. The middle position is where the work happens.

The “GEO is a revolution” camp is overselling

The strong-revolution position holds that AI search has fundamentally broken the SEO playbook and everything must be rebuilt from scratch. This is mostly wrong. The technical fundamentals of SEO haven’t gone anywhere. AI search engines still need to index pages, still weight authoritative sources, still evaluate page quality, still rely on the underlying web graph. Ahrefs found that 76% of AI Overview citations come from sources that already rank in the top 10 organic results. If your SEO is broken, your GEO will be broken downstream of it.

What’s actually new is narrower than the revolution framing suggests. AI Overviews reduce clicks by around 58% (Ahrefs’ February 2026, 300K-keyword study), and CTR drops from roughly 15% to about 8% when an AI Overview is present. That’s a real shift in the funnel. But it’s a shift that sits on top of working SEO, not in place of it. Treating it like a complete reset sells consulting engagements and doesn’t solve client problems.

The hype side and the dismissive side are both selling something. The middle position is where the work happens.

The “GEO is just SEO” camp is being lazy

The dismissive position holds that GEO is a buzzword and competent SEO already covers it. This is also mostly wrong, and it’s a more dangerous error because it sounds like wisdom. There are real techniques that don’t fall out of standard SEO practice and that meaningfully change AI visibility. Cross-source factual consistency across Wikipedia, LinkedIn, Crunchbase, and Amazon author pages isn’t something traditional SEO emphasizes — and it’s a real ranking factor in AI synthesis. Bing indexation isn’t a standard SEO priority either, and it’s a precondition for ChatGPT visibility.

Reddit-as-citation-channel is the clearest example. Reddit has been a part of SEO link strategy for years, but the way it functions in AI search — as a primary source whose threads get extracted into answers, not as a backlink — is structurally different work. Treating that as “just SEO” means running the wrong play. The dismissive camp gets to feel sophisticated while their clients’ brands stay invisible in ChatGPT.

Where I land — and what changes in practice

GEO is best understood as an evolution of SEO. The fundamentals stay; a new layer gets added on top. In practice that means: keep doing technical SEO, content quality, and authority work the same way you always did. Add to it: AI visibility audits across the major engines, off-site authority work weighted toward Reddit and other platforms AI models trust, on-site content restructuring for passage-level extractability, and cross-source factual consistency cleanup. That’s the ARC Method I built around it, and it’s why the framework is 90 days rather than 90 minutes.

I’d argue the more useful debate isn’t whether GEO is real — the data settles that — but how mature the tooling is around it. My honest read is: not very. Most of the GEO platforms shipping right now are repackaging visibility-tracking dashboards that should be free, and selling them at tier-three SaaS prices. The discipline is real. The vendor ecosystem isn’t there yet. Founders should be skeptical of anyone selling a tool before there’s a settled methodology to measure.

Conclusion

Both extremes of the GEO debate are doing the same thing: oversimplifying a partially new problem to sell a clean answer. The honest answer is messier. GEO is real, it’s an evolution rather than a revolution, and the techniques that matter are specific and learnable. If you want the long version with the data, that’s what the book and the podcast are for. If you want the short version, it’s this paragraph.
